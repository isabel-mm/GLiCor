## definición

In corpus linguistics, a **sample** refers to a subset of linguistic data selected from a broader population or domain for the purpose of empirical analysis and inference.

The term is used in two closely related senses:

1. **Sample as a subset of a linguistic population**  
   A collection of texts or text fragments selected to represent a defined discourse domain (e.g., academic research articles, online reviews, parliamentary debates). In this sense, a corpus can be understood as a principled sample of a larger linguistic population.

2. **Sample as a subset within a corpus**  
   A set of linguistic units extracted from an already compiled corpus (e.g., tokens of a grammatical construction, occurrences of a terminological candidate, texts belonging to a specific sub-register). This usage is common in variationist, stylistic, and terminological studies.

A sample is not merely a reduced quantity of data; it is a structured selection whose inferential validity depends on:

- explicit definition of the target population or domain,
- sampling procedure (random, stratified, systematic, convenience-based, etc.),
- sample size relative to the dispersion and frequency of the linguistic feature under investigation.

In quantitative corpus research, the sample constitutes the empirical basis for estimating linguistic parameters (e.g., frequency, dispersion, association measures). Its design directly affects the reliability and generalizability of findings.
