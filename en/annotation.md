## definición

**Annotation** is the methodological procedure by which systematic labels, tags, or metadata are added to a corpus in order to make explicit linguistic, structural, semantic, or pragmatic properties that are not directly observable in raw text.

In corpus linguistics, the term is used in at least two closely related senses:

1. **As a methodological procedure**:  
   Annotation involves the application—manual, semi-automatic, or automatic—of a predefined annotation scheme to units of analysis (tokens, lemmas, phrases, clauses, or full texts). Typical examples include part-of-speech tagging, syntactic parsing, semantic role labeling, discourse annotation, or pragmatic tagging. The process operationalizes theoretical categories in a formal and replicable manner.

2. **As a resulting linguistic resource**:  
   The term may also refer to the enriched corpus itself (annotated corpus), in which one or more layers of structured linguistic information have been added. Such corpora enable advanced querying, quantitative analysis, and computational modeling, often through structured formats such as XML.

Annotation is theory-dependent: every tagging decision presupposes a specific linguistic model and category system. Consequently, annotation is not a neutral technical step but a formal encoding of analytical assumptions. Choices concerning granularity, ambiguity resolution, and inter-annotator agreement directly affect the reliability and interpretability of subsequent analyses.

From a computational perspective, annotation can be understood as the transformation of unstructured textual data into structured data suitable for statistical processing and machine learning applications.
